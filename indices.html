Top 10 indices at a glance

Three considerations decide whether an index is the right NAV anchor for a sleeve: methodology (auction vs continuous mid vs futures TR), adoption (what AUM tracks it), and regulatory status (BMR / IOSCO standing — what the SEC accepts).

The CME CF family (BRRNY + BRR + FBRR-as-mirror) anchors ~$92B of US spot BTC ETF AUM, or ~87% of the $105.9B complex; CoinDesk's family anchors ~$17B (~16%); MarketVector ~$1.3B; Bloomberg Galaxy ~$0.5B. Single-week deviations of ±20–50 bps mean-revert within days. The "broad" indices (CoinDesk BBR with Binance/Bybit/OKX; MVBTC) run slightly wider noise than the regulated-venue-only BRRNY family, because their venue set has more idiosyncratic vol. Takeaway

In gold, the major-index choice is not benchmark vs benchmark — it's spot vs futures. LBMA PM and Bloomberg GOLDS Comdty are interchangeable for NAV purposes. BCOMGC-TR and S&P GSCI Gold TR are a different asset: futures-curve total return that beats spot in low-contango/positive-collateral-yield regimes (recent decade: +2.5%/yr edge over LBMA PM) but lives under §1256 annual mark-to-market that destroys the after-tax case for a 10y US-taxable holder.

In bitcoin, the four spot reference rates are empirically indistinguishable over any holding period that matters. The CME CF family is the institutional default because it cleared UK-BMR first and anchors the regulated futures contract. CoinDesk BBR has broader venue coverage including Binance/Bybit/OKX — useful as a "global" reference. Bloomberg Galaxy and MVBTC exist but anchor very little AUM. Pick your fund on the wrapper layer (ER, custody, AP set), not on the index it tracks.

Underlying data

Read

Modern view (1990 →). Against equities alone, gold's share has cycled in a 4–20% band — ~16% in 1990, ~4% at the 2000 equity peak, ~17% at the 2012 commodity peak, ~10% at the 2021 equity peak. The current 19.9% reading is back at the 2012 cycle high. Against the FSB total global financial assets base, the share is slower-moving: 1–4% for most of the period, with the current snapshot at 5.6% sitting at the high end of the post-1990 range. The denominator captures bank balance sheets and pension reserves that equities + debt securities alone miss.

Long-run view (1900 →). Against world GDP — the only denominator with continuous coverage — gold's share traces the monetary-regime arc. The gold-standard era (1900–1933) holds at ~17–20% of nominal world output. The 1934 dollar devaluation (gold price $20.67 → $35) pushes the ratio toward 36% by 1940. Bretton Woods then compresses it as world GDP grows under a fixed $35 gold price: ~5% by 1960, ~4% by 1970. The 1971 Nixon shock and the 1970s rally take it back to ~19% in 1980, after which the 1980–2000 gold bear and the equity-and-debt expansion of the modern era hold the ratio at 4–6%. The current 27% reading sits above the 1980 cycle high and approaches the 1940 devaluation peak.

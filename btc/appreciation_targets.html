JPMorgan
Nikolaos Panigirtzoglou (MD, Global Markets Strategy)
Global bank
 Volatility-adjusted gold parity (equal risk capital) ~$170k fair value, 6–12mo
~$266k long-term as vol ratio compresses		 A "mechanical exercise." Benchmark BTC's investable cap against the private gold investment market (~$6.2T: ETFs, bars, coins), but adjust for volatility: a dollar in BTC consumes more risk capital than a dollar in gold. At ~1.8× the risk capital of gold, parity = $6.2T ÷ 1.8 → BTC must rise ~⅔ → ~$170k. As the vol ratio fell toward a record ~1.5×, the same gold base supports ~$266k, and BTC flips to a discount vs gold on a risk-adjusted basis.
  • Private gold investment market ≈ $6.2T is the comparable pool
  • BTC & gold compete for the same risk-capital budget
  • Position sizes scale inversely to volatility
  • BTC realized vol trending down toward gold's (1.8× → 1.5×)

Section 2 — Other Approaches & External ModelsBeyond the asset-manager house views: the academic, on-chain, and individual models that define the broader range of how people value Bitcoin long-term. Includes deliberately aggressive advocacy ("pontification") calls — labeled as such.

Model / Voice Approach Implied long-term level How it works Status / caveat Source
Stock-to-Flow (S2F)
"PlanB" (pseudonymous)
 Scarcity ratio 2021-cycle model called ~$100k–$288k; high six figures on scarcity alone Regresses price on the stock-to-flow ratio (existing supply ÷ annual new issuance). Each halving doubles S2F, producing step-changes in modeled value. The S2FX variant places BTC on a cross-asset scarcity line with gold and silver. Hugely influential 2019–21 but widely viewed as invalidated — price collapsed far below the model after 2021. Descriptive, not house-grade. "Modeling Bitcoin Value with Scarcity" 2019
Bitcoin Power Law
Giovanni Santostasi
 Power-law time regression ~$1M in the early-to-mid 2030s (long-run corridor) log(price) grows linearly with log(time since genesis), slope ~5.8 — treats Bitcoin as a network/physical phenomenon on a fixed power-law trajectory, defining a long-run support/resistance corridor rather than a dated point. Strong historical fit; theoretical justification debated. A trajectory/corridor, not a precise forecast. The Bitcoin Power Law Theory
Metcalfe network value
Timothy Peterson, Cane Island
 Network effect Probabilistic floors; ~$1M+ by ~2030 in adoption scenarios Value scales with (active users)² (Metcalfe's Law), with adoption following a Gompertz / virus-spread curve. Yields "Lowest Price Forward" probabilistic price floors rather than upside targets. Published, peer-style methodology; floor-oriented (what BTC is unlikely to fall below). Cane Island / T. Peterson
Rainbow Chart
Blockchaincenter
 Logarithmic-regression bands Sentiment bands — no point target Fits a log-growth curve to price history and colors deviation bands from "fire sale" to "maximum bubble." A positioning/sentiment visual layered on the long-run log trend. Popular heuristic; explicitly not a valuation model (author's own disclaimer). Bitcoin Rainbow Chart
On-chain valuation
MVRV · NVT · Realized Cap · Thermocap
 On-chain ratios Relative cycle signals, not absolute targets Compare market cap to realized cap (MVRV), to transaction volume (NVT), or to cumulative miner revenue (Thermocap) to gauge over/undervaluation within a cycle and flag tops/bottoms. Timing & relative-value tools — not long-term price forecasts. Glassnode / Coin Metrics / Willy Woo
Cost of production
Energy-value / miner breakeven
 Production-cost floor A moving floor that tracks energy + hardware cost/coin The marginal cost to mine a coin (electricity + rig depreciation) acts as a soft price floor; "energy value" models translate hash-rate and power cost into an implied fair value. Floor logic; contested as an upper-bound valuation tool. Cambridge CBECI / energy-value models

Reading the table. The cleanest divide is methodological, not numerical: BlackRock and Binance Research refuse a price target — BlackRock reframes it as "how much risk does a 1–2% sleeve add" and Binance as macro-cycle direction. Everyone else commits to a number, but via three distinct engines — network-adoption math (Fidelity's Metcalfe/S-curve), addressable-market build-ups (ARK's use-case TAMs, VanEck's monetary model, Bitwise/Grayscale/Galaxy's store-of-value share of gold), and a volatility-adjusted gold-parity formula (JPMorgan). Gold is the shared anchor for most — but they use it differently: a TAM to capture a share of (Grayscale, Galaxy, Bitwise), a qualitative debasement narrative (Standard Chartered), or a precise risk-capital divisor (JPMorgan).

Caveats: targets dated to ~mid-2026 and several are live-moving (Standard Chartered cut twice; ARK revised through 2025–26).
